# Labelling rubric

Every pair is one job (from `data/jobs.csv`) and one CV. Score how well the CV fits the job's **must-haves**.

## Scale

| Score | Meaning | Rule (each job has 3 must-haves) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Strong fit | All 3 must-haves clearly met |
| 1 | Partial fit | 2 of 3 met, or all 3 only partly met |
| 0 | Not a fit | 0 or 1 met |

## General rules

1. **Judge only what the CV says.** Don't assume skills it doesn't mention.
2. **Job titles count as evidence for standard duties of that title**, not for specific tools. "Staff Accountant" counts as reconciliations and ledger work; it doesn't count as "uses SAP".
3. **Years of experience count only in relevant roles.** Overlapping roles count once. If dates are missing, don't count that role toward years.
4. **A skill listed with no role or task behind it counts as partly met**, not met. Many of these CVs have long keyword lists.
5. **A degree in progress counts as partly met.**
6. **Nice-to-haves never change the score.** They're for ranking later, not for labelling.
7. **Length and polish don't matter.** A short CV that meets all three must-haves is a 2.
8. **When torn between two scores, pick the lower one and write a note.** One consistent rule beats deciding case by case.
